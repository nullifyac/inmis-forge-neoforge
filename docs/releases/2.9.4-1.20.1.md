Inmis 2.9.4 (Forge 1.20.1)

Update Inmis on both client and server together: this release changes the backpack menu protocol. Custom backpack item names and tier order must still agree on both sides; storage dimensions may differ.

- Funnelling now enforces backpack storage rules, including rejecting other backpacks and prohibited shulker boxes. Partial pickups preserve the remainder.
- Previously nested backpacks remain extractable. The active backpack slot stays protected against hotbar/offhand swaps while unrelated backpacks remain movable.
- Backpack menus use server dimensions when the client has different slot settings, and augments share the open menu's contents so later clicks do not overwrite their changes.
- Reducing configured capacity or crafting an upgrade preserves occupied overflow in extraction-only recovery slots.
- Backpacked imports preserve valid overflow and retain legacy source data when decoding or slot validation fails.
- Immortal can consume a backpack totem while a shield or other item occupies the offhand. Held totems keep priority, and disabled augments and bypass damage follow vanilla behavior.
- Fixed death spilling for vanilla inventory, armor and offhand, plus backpacks selected for dropping from Curios. Native retention/destruction rules, keep-inventory behavior, Vanishing Curse and death-drop cancellation are preserved.
- Disabling trinket compatibility blocks new equipment admission through Curios, while already equipped backpacks remain removable.
- Invalid existing configurations are preserved and fail loading with a clear error instead of being overwritten with defaults.
- Fixed early backpack item construction during mod loading, avoiding registration while item registries are frozen.

Verified with Minecraft GameTest servers on all four supported versions, including native Curios and Accessories death-drop rules and cancellation in their supported builds. NeoForge also passed client/server transfers and restart persistence checks with no integrations, Curios, Accessories and both, including differing client/server dimensions and reduced capacity.

The reported freezes and long rollback have not been reproduced. These changes address specific storage and synchronization defects; short clean-restart checks do not establish crash recovery or long-session stability.
